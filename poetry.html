Three poems about the strange, the ordinary, and the things that wait beneath the surface.

Selected writings · 2026

IN THIS ARCHIVE: itching to be born · saving a squirrel · Spring Has Come Early

01 / POEM

itching to be born

two crows flitter away
from barren branches
since the scheming caterpillar
devoured all the leaves

and deer stomp their hooves
at squirrels who are terrified
to share their treasure

what chimerical creature etches
its name upon our land
or dresses as a stately man in disguise
defending lions from their prey

and what belly beast of hate
stands in wait
just itching to be born

02 / POEM

saving a squirrel

early morning drive
pale blue sky kisses
sad gray pavement
trees tower above
guard their own tiny houses

squirrel scurries gently
brakes slam jarringly
people begin to honk
squirrel gets away

could have kept going
chose to stop anyway

03 / POEM

Spring Has Come Early

The world is so full of wonder that to leave it would be to forgo an experience of infinite marvels.

On a warm March night I sit on the steps—
The stone cool on my hands.

I sit and watch the world around me,
As the soft breeze blows through my hair.

I see dark, leafless trees looming above
Against a backdrop of white, glowing dots in the sky.
Dogs bark in the distance—
I like to think they’re laughing.

The air smells of clean, peaceful spring grass,
Warm and damp beneath the cold moon.

Then appears a man before me.
So familiar yet still a stranger.
He asks if he may sit here too.

I scoot right over and let him sit.
He remarks that “Spring has come early,”
Then inquires if I’m excited.

I reply, simply, yes.

He sits and stares down toward the grass.
We sit in silence for a minute we wish would last forever.

He stands and takes a few measured steps,
Then turns to face me.
“Why are you afraid of death?” he asks.
Then I look to the stars,
And tell him—

“That is why.”
